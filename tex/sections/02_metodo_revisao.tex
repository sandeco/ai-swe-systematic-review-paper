\section{Review Method}

The Introduction framed AI-assisted development frameworks as a layer that organizes the work of whoever uses a development agent, and argued that comparing them requires examining the function of each mechanism rather than surface metrics such as command counts or popularity. Investigating this object imposes a methodological constraint: the most relevant frameworks circulate outside academic venues, in open repositories, official documentation, and community kits, so a review restricted to indexed papers would miss them. We therefore conducted the review as a multivocal literature review (MLR), which combines, under a single protocol, the rigor of the Kitchenham systematic review for formal literature with the guidelines of Garousi, Felderer, and Mäntylä for grey literature \cite{kitchenham2009slr,garousi2019mlr}. The cutoff date was May 26, 2026, and the coverage window ran from 2018 to 2026.

The two corpora play distinct and complementary roles. The formal literature, made of papers and preprints retrieved from academic databases, grounds the analytical dimensions of the taxonomy and establishes the state of knowledge on agents, specification, validation, and risk. The grey literature, made of the primary sources of the support frameworks, supplies the objects that those dimensions characterize. The protocol was fixed before execution and defines the research questions, the sources consulted, the inclusion and exclusion criteria, the extraction schema, and the snowballing strategy. Figure~\ref{fig:metodologia} summarizes the survey flow for the formal literature, from identification to reporting, with the real counts at each step.

The review is guided by the five research questions presented in the Introduction (RQ1 through RQ5). RQ5, about the gaps that remain for comparing, evaluating, and governing these frameworks in real projects, is a synthesis-level question answered transversally in the Research Agenda, which is why the extraction matrix records only RQ1 through RQ4 per study.

The search followed two strategies. For the formal literature, we queried arXiv, the ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Crossref, OpenAlex, and Semantic Scholar in an automated way, with Scopus and Web of Science reached through open metadata via OpenAlex and Google Scholar used for citation checking. The descriptors were organized into four conceptual blocks (AI-assisted development, agentic software engineering, specification as artifact, and organization by workflows), with Boolean strings adapted to each database and documented in the protocol. For the grey literature, where string search does not apply, we used a directed search of the frameworks' primary sources (official documentation, repositories, and product blogs), with its own scope and traction criteria detailed below; the extraction schema, however, was the same in both strands.

The eligibility criteria for the formal literature were fixed in the protocol and are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:criterios}. The decision rule is aggregate: a source that fails criterion I1 is excluded; among sources that satisfy I1, only those that satisfy at least two of I2, I3, and I4 are included. Exclusion reasons were recorded operationally for each discarded source, allowing an auditable account of the discards in each pass.

For the formal literature, the multi-database search returned 100 raw records, reduced to 85 candidates after deduplication by DOI and title, the 2018 to 2026 temporal filter, and the inclusion of one directed seed that belonged to the initial scope but was absent from the automatic retrieval. These 85 candidates went through a first title-and-abstract screening, which advanced 28 studies to full-text reading and excluded 57. In the second pass, the 28 full texts were read in full under the same criteria, yielding 25 included studies and 3 excluded. The counts and exclusion reasons for both passes were recorded in an append-only log for auditing.

From the 25 included studies, we applied backward and forward snowballing following Wohlin's guidelines \cite{wohlin2014snowballing}, with one depth level and a limit of three rounds, each seeded by the previous round's inclusions. The three rounds generated 69, 50, and 30 new candidates, respectively; of these, 9, 5, and 4 advanced to full text and 6, 3, and 3 were included, and the forward search returned no eligible citations in any round. In total, 149 additional candidates were examined and 12 new studies incorporated, consolidating the final corpus at 37 included studies. One source approved at the third-round screening lacked full text at the cutoff date and was kept as an operational pending item, not an exclusion.

The review was conducted as multivocal for a coverage reason. A large share of the frameworks that organize development on top of an agent circulate in unindexed primary sources, such as official documentation, repositories, and product blogs, so restricting the review to indexed papers would leave that layer out. The grey-literature strand was therefore gathered by a directed search of those primary sources, under the same extraction schema as the formal literature but with its own scope and traction criteria. The in-depth characterization of support frameworks for development on top of an agent, such as GitHub Spec Kit, OpenSpec, BMAD Method, Get Shit Done, and Spec Kitty, is reported in a companion study dedicated to that scope. This article concentrates on the formal corpus of 37 studies, which establishes the structure and the tensions of the field. Reversa belongs to that formal corpus because it entered as a directed seed in the search.

Ten structured fields were extracted from each included study: name, nature, origin, artifacts, roles, execution, validation, portability, evidence, and risks. Consolidating the extraction into a matrix allowed the studies to be compared uniformly. The synthesis followed a hybrid thematic approach: inductive themes were identified from the extraction records and then confronted with the six-dimension taxonomy proposed in the review (specification, context, roles, execution, validation, and portability), reporting the overlap, the thinly covered dimensions, and the themes that emerge outside the taxonomy.

Finally, note that a formal quality assessment by checklist was not applied in this round: the strength of the evidence is classified by source category, distinguishing empirical study, conceptual paper, official documentation, and public repository. Because tool repositories and documentation change quickly, observations about versions, integrations, releases, or commands should be read as a snapshot of the corpus consulted at the cutoff date, not as stable properties of the frameworks. The remaining limitations that bound the reading of the results are consolidated in the Review Limitations section.
